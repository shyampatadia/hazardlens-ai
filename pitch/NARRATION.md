# HazardLens AI — VC pitch narration

Timed to `out/hazardlens-pitch-narrated.mp4` (2:30). The rendered video already
carries a synthetic voiceover generated by `narrate.py`. This is the same script
written out, so you can record it in your own voice instead — which is what we
would use in front of an actual investor.

Record it, then mux:

```powershell
ffmpeg -i out/hazardlens-pitch.mp4 -i narration.m4a -c:v copy -c:a aac -shortest out/pitch-final.mp4
```

Pace is about 145 words per minute. Let each visual land before moving on.


### 0:00 — Hook (14s)

> Every workplace has a fire exit. Most of the time, something is parked in front of it. Safety inspections are manual, infrequent, and the hazard is hiding in plain sight.

### 0:14 — Product (15s)

> This is HazardLens AI. Upload a photo of your site, type a safety question in plain English, and get that photo back with the objects that matter boxed and labelled.

### 0:29 — Live demo (22s)

> Here it is running. The inspector asks: find anything blocking the emergency exit. HazardLens reads the photo, works out what counts as an obstruction, and marks every one it finds. Four obstructions, in under four seconds, in front of a fire door with an alarm on it. No checklist, no training, no specialist.

### 0:51 — Differentiator (17s)

> Here is what makes that hard. Every other tool makes you pick what to look for from a list, so the inspector has to already know what the hazard is. HazardLens works it out from the question itself.

### 1:08 — Speed (20s)

> Four seconds per inspection. That number is the whole product. Today a safety walkthrough is a clipboard and a quarterly calendar reminder. At four seconds it becomes a photo from a phone, every shift, taken by anyone on site. Our real competition is not another tool, it is the inspection that never happened.

### 1:28 — Unit economics (21s)

> Six hundredths of a cent per inspection. We did not model that, we ran the product and priced what it actually consumed. A thousand customers doing ten thousand inspections a month costs us six dollars and forty cents to serve. Infrastructure is a rounding error against any subscription price, and it scales with usage, not headcount.

### 1:49 — Reliability (20s)

> Safety tooling that is down is worse than no safety tooling, because people stop trusting it. So every inspection has a second path. If our fastest route degrades, the work reroutes to our own infrastructure automatically and the customer never sees the switch. We built that before we needed it.

### 2:10 — Privacy and close (20s)

> And that second path is our moat. Safety photos show employees, layouts, and evidence something is wrong. Regulated customers cannot let those leave the building. We run the whole product inside their walls. That turns the buyers everyone else walks away from into our enterprise tier. HazardLens AI. See the hazard before it costs you.

- The strongest investor line is at 1:09: *our real competition is not another
  tool, it is the inspection that never happened*. Pause before it.
- 1:29 is the margin story. Say the numbers slowly, they are small and easy to
  miss.
- Nothing in this script names a model or a vendor on purpose. An investor is
  buying the outcome, not the architecture. The technical detail lives in
  `report.md` for anyone doing diligence.
- Everything on screen is real: detections come from running the detector on
  `test_images/test_image_2.jpg`, and the latency and cost figures come from a
  live run against the deployed Space.
